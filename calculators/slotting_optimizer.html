SKU Slotting Optimizer

Pre-loaded with customer data from this SE engagement — 5,000 SKUs, 60-day OB history

What this calculator does: Classifies every SKU by ABC velocity tier based on pick frequency, compares current slotted zone to the optimal zone for that velocity tier, and quantifies the walk distance waste created by slotting drift. The output drives the pre-deployment re-slot recommendation and sets up a clean zone structure for Locus robot assignment.

Why this matters for robot deployment: You cannot size or zone-assign robots effectively into a sub-optimal slot structure. Origin robots are zone-restricted — if the fastest SKUs are in the wrong zones, the robot assignment is wrong by definition. Fix slotting first, then deploy.

Future Calculator Work

Items identified for future development to make this calculator more precise and broadly applicable across customer scenarios.

1 — Robot Mode Toggle (Human / Origin / Array)

The current calculator optimizes purely for human picking efficiency. The slotting criteria shift by robot type: Origin mode increases weight on zone clustering density (tighter clusters = more robot efficiency); Array mode removes ergonomic tier as a factor entirely (Array reaches all 5 levels equally) and adjusts the distance model for robot travel speed vs. human walking pace. A mode toggle would recalculate drift count and re-slot recommendations based on the target deployment.

2 — Aisle-Level Granularity

Zone-level distances are a reasonable approximation but introduce error for large zones. Replacing zone distances with aisle-level distances (pulled from WMS or measured on-site) would improve the walk distance model and make re-slot priority rankings more accurate. Particularly important for Array deployment planning where robot travel path efficiency matters at the aisle level.

3 — SE-Adjustable SKU-Level Overrides

Some SKUs have operational constraints that override velocity-based slotting — vendor agreements, temperature requirements, hazmat zones, or items that must stay together for fulfillment reasons. An override field would let the SE flag individual SKUs as location-locked so they are excluded from the re-slot recommendation without affecting the overall drift metrics.

4 — SKU Affinity Modeling

Frequently co-ordered SKUs (ordered together in the same shipment) should ideally be slotted in proximity to minimize within-batch travel. Mining the OB transaction data for SKU co-occurrence pairs and incorporating affinity scores into zone placement would further reduce picks-per-batch travel time, beyond what ABC tiering alone achieves.
